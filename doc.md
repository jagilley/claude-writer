# THE ELIXIR

## A Pro Wrestling Musical

### Adapted from Donizetti's "L'elisir d'amore"

* * *

## CONCEPT & TONE

The show leans into wrestling's inherent sincerity-within-absurdity. We play everything STRAIGHT—the emotions are real, the stakes are real—but we acknowledge the cheese with a knowing wink. Think: if the characters in a Hallmark movie knew they were in a Hallmark movie but committed anyway because the feelings are genuine.

The central question: **If a placebo works, is it fake?** If believing in something false makes you genuinely better, what does that say about the nature of performance, confidence, and love?

Dulcamara knows he's selling snake oil. Nemorino knows, on some level, that magic isn't real. But kayfabe has power. The work can become the shoot. That's not a contradiction—that's wrestling.

Nothing showing up

* * *

## SETTING

**AMORE PRO WRESTLING** — a beloved but struggling independent promotion somewhere in the American heartland. It's the kind of place where the arena is a converted warehouse, the production values are held together with duct tape and dreams, and everyone on the roster knows everyone else's real name.

The promotion is at a crossroads: stay independent and probably die slowly, or get absorbed by **TITAN WRESTLING ENTERTAINMENT**, the soulless corporate giant that's been buying up territories.

* * *

## CHARACTERS

### NEMORINO (NEMO RENO)

_Babyface. Lower-card. The guy who opens the show._

Nemo has been wrestling for Amore Pro for six years. He's technically solid—good fundamentals, safe worker, shows up early and stays late. But he has no gimmick, no presence, no "it factor." His entrance music is generic. His tights are plain black. When he wrestles, the crowd goes to get beer.

He is desperately, hopelessly, pathetically in love with Adina. Everyone knows it. He's not subtle. He writes her poetry (bad poetry). He brings her coffee (she doesn't drink coffee). He once tried to save her from a run-in and got powerbombed for his trouble.

He is a golden retriever who somehow learned a snapmare.

### ADINA

_The Ace. Women's Champion. The reason people buy tickets._

Adina is the total package: she can work, she can talk, she's over with the crowd, and she knows her wrestling history better than anyone. Her promos reference Mildred Burke and The Fabulous Moolah. She's obsessed with the legendary romances of wrestling—the real ones, the kayfabe ones, she doesn't care, she loves the DRAMA of it all.

She knows Nemorino loves her. She finds it... sweet? Annoying? Both? She doesn't take it seriously because he doesn't seem serious—he's so EARNEST it feels like a bit. She's waiting for someone with PRESENCE. Someone whose love would feel like a storyline.

She's also, on some level she won't admit, terrified of the promotion folding. This is her home. These are her people.

### SERGEANT BELCORE

_Heel. Corporate. The Invader._

Belcore is a mid-card talent from Titan Wrestling Entertainment sent to "scout" Amore Pro for acquisition—which everyone knows means "absorb the talent, kill the brand." He wrestles in military fatigues. His finisher is called the Court Martial. He has abs you could grate cheese on and the personality of a protein shake.

He's not EVIL, exactly. He genuinely believes corporate wrestling is better—more money, more exposure, better healthcare. He thinks he's SAVING these people from their sinking ship. He wants to sign Adina because she's talented, but also because acquiring the local ace is a power move.

He's a heel, but the kind of heel who doesn't know he's the heel. He thinks he's the protagonist.

### DR. DULCAMARA

_Traveling Manager. Gimmick Salesman. Carny Legend._

Dulcamara has been in the wrestling business for forty years. He's been a manager, a promoter, a territory owner, a color commentator, and allegedly a wrestler himself (though no footage exists). He travels from promotion to promotion selling his services: new gimmicks, character consultations, "the secrets of the old-timers."

He is, to be clear, a complete fraud. His advice is mostly nonsense. His gimmicks are recycled. His "secrets" are things you could learn from YouTube.

BUT: he's been around so long that he's become mythical. People WANT to believe. And sometimes—not always, but sometimes—wrestlers who buy his snake oil actually DO get better. Confidence is a hell of a drug.

He speaks like a tent revival preacher crossed with a used car salesman. He has a PhD in nothing but insists on the honorific.

### THE ROSTER (Ensemble)

-   **GIANNETTA** — Veteran of the women's division, locker room gossip, secretly has a heart of gold
    
-   **VARIOUS WRESTLERS** — Faces and heels, the community of Amore Pro

## SYNOPSIS

### ACT ONE

**SCENE 1: THE WAREHOUSE (Opening Number: "Amore Pro")**

Lights up on the Amore Pro Wrestling warehouse. The roster is preparing for tonight's show—stretching, taping wrists, arguing about finishes. The opening number establishes the world: this is a family, these people love each other, the pay sucks, the building has rats, they do it anyway because wrestling is a calling.

We meet NEMORINO (NEMO), helping set up the ring even though it's not his job. We meet ADINA, champion belt over her shoulder, holding court while reading a book about wrestling history.

Nemo watches Adina from across the room. Giannetta notices. The whole locker room notices. It's embarrassing for everyone.

**SCENE 2: "TRISTAN AND ISOLDE" (Adina's Song)**

Adina reads aloud from her book, telling the story of a legendary wrestling romance—a kayfabe love angle that became real, changed the business, became immortal. She's obsessed with this stuff: Eddie and Liz, Macho and Elizabeth, Triple H and Stephanie (she's not picky about whether it's healthy, she just wants EPIC).

The song is romantic and slightly ironic—she knows she's being dramatic, she's performing even now, but the longing underneath is real. She wants a love that feels like a main event.

Nemo tries to approach her. He's brought her flowers (from the gas station). He stumbles through a confession that's so awkward the crowd physically cringes. Adina lets him down gently—she likes him, sure, but he's not... he's not a STORY. He's not someone she can believe in.

**SCENE 3: "THE TITAN HAS LANDED" (Belcore's Entrance)**

The back door slams open. SERGEANT BELCORE enters with full heel energy—pyro if possible, definitely his own entrance music, probably accompanied by a drone camera operator.

He's here from Titan Wrestling Entertainment. He's here to make offers. He's here, specifically, for Adina—they want her on the main roster, and he's been sent to close the deal.

His song is a banger heel promo: corporate buzzwords, thinly veiled threats, absolute confidence. He negs the warehouse, negs the roster, compliments Adina in a way that's more acquisition than affection.

Adina is... intrigued? He's awful, but he's CONFIDENT. He believes in himself so completely. That's attractive, in a car-crash kind of way.

Belcore announces that Titan is offering Adina a developmental contract. She has until the end of the weekend to decide. Also, he'll be wrestling on tonight's show to "demonstrate Titan-level quality." (The roster bristles.)

**SCENE 4: DULCAMARA ARRIVES (The Snake Oil Song)**

A commotion at the door. DR. DULCAMARA has arrived, unannounced, in a vehicle that can only be described as "if a van could wear a sequined jacket."

He launches into his number—a patter song in the style of "Ya Got Trouble" from _Music Man_ but about wrestling gimmicks. He name-drops every territory, every legend (he managed them all, supposedly), every transformation (he created them all, allegedly). He's selling:

-   New gimmicks, new personas
    
-   Entrance music consultation
    
-   Promo coaching
    
-   And, for the truly committed: **THE ELIXIR**
    

The Elixir, he explains, is a secret formula passed down through generations of wrestling royalty. One sip before your match, and you will become MAGNETIC. Undeniable. Someone the crowd—and anyone you desire—cannot look away from.

It is, obviously, just grape Gatorade mixed with pre-workout.

The roster is skeptical but entertained. Dulcamara starts making deals—a new gimmick here, some promo tips there.

**SCENE 5: THE PURCHASE (Nemo's Desperate Hope)**

Nemorino approaches Dulcamara privately. He asks about the Elixir. Is it real? Can it really make someone... lovable?

Dulcamara sizes him up—this guy is a mark, a true believer, a perfect customer. He spins a tale about the Elixir: it doesn't just give you confidence, it gives you PRESENCE. It makes people see you differently. It takes 24 hours to kick in (conveniently giving Dulcamara time to leave town).

The price is steep—Nemorino's entire savings, basically. But he pays. He believes.

Dulcamara gives him THE ELIXIR: a small bottle with a hand-written label, very obviously homemade. Nemorino drinks it like it's holy water.

Song: **"Twenty-Four Hours"** — Nemorino imagining his transformed future, intercut with Dulcamara counting the money, intercut with the audience being invited to hope along with this sweet idiot.

**SCENE 6: TRANSFORMATION (The Elixir "Works")**

The next day. Something has shifted in Nemorino—not magic, just confidence. He BELIEVES now. He walks differently. He talks differently. He's decided to stop chasing Adina and just... be someone worth chasing.

This backfires in the best way. Adina notices him NOT paying attention to her. She's confused. Annoyed. Intrigued?

Nemorino, misreading the Elixir's "instructions" (Dulcamara made some things up), decides he needs to play HARD TO GET. He needs to be MYSTERIOUS. He adopts a new persona mid-show—darker gear, brooding attitude, a finishing move he calls THE REMEDY.

It's cheesy as hell. The crowd doesn't know if it's a bit. Adina doesn't know if it's a bit. Nemo himself isn't sure if it's a bit.

But here's the thing: **it kind of works?** His match that night is actually good. The crowd is actually engaged. Something about committing fully—even to something absurd—has unlocked something.

**SCENE 7: THE CONTRACT (Act One Finale)**

Belcore makes his move. He presents Adina with the Titan contract: main roster, big money, national TV. All she has to do is leave Amore Pro behind.

Adina looks at Nemorino, who is (per the Elixir's instructions) ignoring her completely. She's FURIOUS. She thought—she doesn't know what she thought. But she didn't think he'd just MOVE ON.

In a moment of pique, she signs the contract. She's leaving. With Belcore. Tomorrow.

Nemorino's confidence shatters. The Elixir isn't working fast enough. He needs MORE.

He finds Dulcamara, who's packing up to leave. "I need more Elixir. I'll pay anything."

Dulcamara shrugs. "Kid, I'm tapped out. Unless you've got something else to trade..."

Belcore appears. He's looking for enhancement talent—jobbers willing to sign contracts with Titan in exchange for cash. Wrestlers who'll do the jobs, take the losses, make the stars look good.

Nemorino approaches Belcore. He'll sign. He'll be a jobber. He just needs the money NOW.

Song: **"Sign on the Line"** — Nemorino selling his future for grape Gatorade, while Adina watches in horror (but doesn't intervene, because she's angry, because she's confused, because she doesn't understand why she cares this much).

He signs. He gets his money. He buys more Elixir.

**BLACKOUT. END OF ACT ONE.**

* * *

### ACT TWO

**SCENE 1: THE VIRAL MOMENT (Gossip Spreads)**

The next morning. Giannetta has NEWS.

She's been on wrestling Twitter. She's been refreshing ALL NIGHT. And she has discovered something INCREDIBLE:

Nemorino's match from last night—the one where he debuted "The Remedy" persona—has gone **VIRAL**. Not because of his moveset, but because of a single moment: after nearly losing, he grabbed the mic and cut an unscripted promo. Raw. Emotional. Real. The kind of moment that makes people forget they're watching a work.

The clip has been shared thousands of times. Podcasters are calling him "the most authentic thing in wrestling." Someone spotted a talent scout from a major promotion in the building last night. A wrestling journalist with 200K followers tweeted: "Remember this name."

In wrestling terms, Nemorino just became the hottest free agent in the building.

Song: **"Did You Hear?"** — A gossip number where the news spreads through the roster at lightning speed, everyone scrolling through their phones, reading tweets aloud, watching reaction videos. The staging should be contemporary—screens lighting up faces, notifications dinging, everyone re-evaluating Nemorino in real time. This number should be funny, frantic, and staged like a social media explosion.

**SCENE 2: THE ELIXIR "WORKS" (Nemorino Ascendant)**

Nemorino enters, having consumed more Elixir, ready for whatever happens. He is IMMEDIATELY swarmed by wrestlers who want to:

-   Tag team with him
    
-   Manage him
    
-   Train with him
    
-   Date him
    

He is baffled. He is delighted. THE ELIXIR IS WORKING.

(He does not know about the viral clip. He thinks it's the Elixir.)

Song: **"It's Working"** — A comic number where Nemorino attributes his new popularity to the magic potion, while the audience knows the truth. Physical comedy, flirtation, escalating absurdity. Maybe he gets lifted onto shoulders. Maybe someone offers him their championship opportunity. He's living his best life.

**SCENE 3: ADINA WATCHES**

Adina, still contracted to leave with Belcore, watches Nemorino's sudden popularity with FEELINGS she cannot name.

She's supposed to be leaving. She has what she wanted—the big league, the spotlight, the trajectory. So why can't she stop watching this idiot drink his fake potion and get fawned over?

Giannetta approaches. She tells Adina about the viral clip—that's why everyone's suddenly interested in Nemo. One authentic moment and suddenly he's the hottest thing in wrestling Twitter.

Adina's response surprises her: she's not relieved that it's just internet hype, she's ANGRY. These people don't actually see him. They see a clip. They see clout. They see what they can get.

She sees HIM. She's always seen him. She just didn't... she didn't think it was a STORY. But it is. It's a story about someone who believed in something so hard it changed him. That's the most wrestling thing there is.

**SCENE 4: "UNA FURTIVA LAGRIMA" EQUIVALENT ("One Single Tear")**

The emotional climax.

Nemorino, alone, watching from a distance as Adina talks with Belcore. He sees something he didn't expect:

Adina is upset. Adina is CRYING. One single tear, quickly wiped away, but unmistakable.

She's crying about HIM.

This is the big ballad. Nemorino realizes: she feels something. Even if it's just regret, even if it's just nostalgia, she FEELS something. That's more than he had before. That's proof that this wasn't all in his head.

The song is sincere. No irony. Just a man realizing he might be loved, and being terrified and grateful in equal measure. This is the "shoot" moment—the emotional truth beneath all the kayfabe.

(In the opera, this aria is one of the most famous tenor arias ever written. The wrestling musical version needs to be a SHOWSTOPPER. The kind of song where the audience forgets they're in a warehouse watching fake fighting. The kind of song that makes people cry into their overpriced beer.)

**SCENE 5: ADINA'S CHOICE**

Adina finds Dulcamara, who's FINALLY about to leave town.

She asks him about the Elixir. Is it real?

Dulcamara, for once, is honest—or as honest as he gets. "Lady, nothing I sell is real. But that kid believed in it so hard he made it real. That's not my magic. That's his."

Adina makes a decision.

She goes to Belcore. She wants to buy out Nemorino's contract.

Belcore laughs. Why would she do that? The kid signed fair and square.

Adina offers: herself. Her contract with Titan. She'll still sign—but only if Belcore releases Nemorino. The company gets their acquisition; Nemorino gets his freedom.

Belcore considers. This is actually a BETTER deal for him—Adina's worth ten Nemorinos. He agrees.

Song: **"The Buyout"** — A tense negotiation number, Adina trading her future for Nemorino's freedom, Belcore smugly agreeing, the ensemble watching in shock.

**SCENE 6: THE CONFESSION**

Adina finds Nemorino. She hands him his torn-up contract.

"You're free. You don't owe them anything."

Nemorino is confused. Why would she—what does this—

Adina, for the first time, drops the performance. No kayfabe. No story. Just her.

"You believed in something so stupid it should have been embarrassing. But you believed so hard you made it true. I've been waiting for a love that felt like a main event, but I already had it. I was just too much of a mark for the wrong things to see it."

She loves him. She's choosing him. She's choosing to stay.

Nemorino: "I should tell you something. The Elixir—"

Adina: "I know. It's grape Gatorade."

Nemorino: "...it's really good grape Gatorade."

They kiss. The crowd POPS.

**SCENE 7: FINALE ("THE ELIXIR WORKS")**

The whole roster assembles. Belcore, having gotten what he came for (Adina's contract, which she honors—she'll do a run in the big leagues and come back), departs without rancor. He's a heel, but he's a professional.

Dulcamara reappears for a final bow. He addresses the crowd directly:

"Ladies and gentlemen, you have witnessed a MIRACLE. My elixir—my COMPLETELY LEGITIMATE elixir—has united two hearts. And it can do the same for YOU."

He starts selling bottles to the audience. This is interactive—he's working the crowd, getting people to buy in (metaphorically or literally, depending on how committed you want to get to the bit).

The final number brings everyone together: a celebration of belief, of commitment to the bit, of loving something enough to make it real. The ensemble reprises the best musical moments. Nemorino and Adina are together. The promotion survives. Nothing is certain, but tonight, in this warehouse, magic is real because we say it is.

Final image: Nemorino and Adina in the ring, the roster around them, the crowd on their feet.

Dulcamara, exiting with a suitcase full of cash and empty bottles: "THE ELIXIR WORKS!"

**BLACKOUT. END OF SHOW.**

## NOTES ON STAGING & CROWD INTERACTION

**The venue** should feel like a real wrestling show. Ring in the center, audience on all sides or three sides, minimal fourth wall. The crowd should be encouraged to cheer faces and boo heels from the beginning.

**The matches** are choreographed musical numbers. Think: "Cell Block Tango" if it involved suplexes. The wrestling IS the dancing.

**Dulcamara's sales pitches** can be genuinely interactive—he can read the crowd, riff, adjust. This role needs someone with improv chops and carny energy.

**The Elixir itself** should be a running prop. Nemorino drinks it CONSTANTLY. It should get a reaction every time—cheers, groans, chants of "DRINK! DRINK! DRINK!" if we're lucky.

**"One Single Tear"** needs to land in SILENCE. This is the moment we stop being a wrestling show and become a love story. The crowd should forget to cheer. Then, when it ends, the catharsis should be enormous.

* * *

## THEMATIC SUMMARY

The show asks: **What's the difference between a placebo and a miracle?**

Nemorino's journey is about the power of belief. The Elixir is fake, but his transformation is real. Was it the Elixir? Was it confidence? Was it the permission to become someone new? Does it matter?

Wrestling runs on this tension: everyone knows it's "fake," but the athleticism is real, the injuries are real, the emotions are real. Kayfabe isn't a lie—it's a shared agreement to believe in something together.

Love is the same way. It's a story we tell until it becomes true.

**The Elixir works because Nemorino works. And that's not a con. That's magic.**
